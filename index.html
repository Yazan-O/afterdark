See the street after dark

At night a normal camera goes dark and smears motion. An event camera, a brain-inspired sensor, keeps seeing anything that moves. Its output looks nothing like a photo, yet Meta SAM 3.1 finds the people in it, and Meta Muse Image turns it back into a picture of the street.

A camera that only sees change

An event camera, also called a neuromorphic or brain-inspired camera, works like the eye's retina. Every pixel fires on its own the moment brightness changes, in microseconds, across a far wider range of light than a normal camera. Its output is a stream of sparse flickers, not pictures.

That is why it keeps working where a normal camera struggles: in the dark, next to a headlight, and on anything that moves fast, with no motion blur.

  • Each pixel fires on changeA moving person lights up. A still wall stays quiet
  • Bright and dark in one viewStreet lamps and deep shadow at the same moment
  • Microsecond timingNo frames to wait for, no blur from motion

The catch: almost no AI can read flickers

Vision models are trained on pictures. Event research builds its own pipeline, and every step is special-purpose

Who it is for

Night perception teams. People building vision for cars and robots that work after dark. One open Meta model now reads both sensors

Event camera researchers. Groups with event data and no event-trained models. Meta SAM 3.1 and Meta Muse Image work on it out of the box

Brain-inspired computing. Event cameras pair naturally with spiking neural networks, which compute with spikes the way these sensors report them
